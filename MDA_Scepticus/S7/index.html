COROLLA WORTH ?

Empirical Exercise VII  ·  Multivariate Data Analysis

Master Scepticus wants to sell his old Toyota Corolla, and he has a rule of thumb: a Corolla loses 200 euros of value every month. The prices of 1,419 second-hand Corollas will judge him. You will start with a simple regression on the age, then explain the price with nine characteristics, check the assumptions of the model, add the square of the age, choose the best model, and test two constraints at once with an F-test.

WINDOW 1 · EXCELDownload ToyotaCorolla_with_data_Models.xlsx with the DATA (XLSX) button at the top and open it in Excel. Compute every number and draw every plot there, and keep your workbook: each question builds on the previous one.
WINDOW 2 · THIS GAMEMaster Scepticus asks the questions, one after the other. Bring back the numbers, read your plots, and defend your conclusions in front of him. The game never computes anything for you.

No answer is ever shown in this exercise. Every question has a HINT button, with an example Excel formula, sometimes with ??? for you to fill in. Keep trying until the Master accepts your answer.

Type numbers with a dot or a comma as the decimal separator, e.g., 36.18 or 36,18. Give two decimals: your answer is accepted within 0.01 of the exact value. Very small or very large numbers can be typed as Excel displays them, e.g., -8.54E-05 or 2.10E+09, and are accepted within 1%. Counts must be exact.

EXCEL QUICK REFERENCE

The file has four sheets. ToyotaCorolla holds the raw data: column A the price in euros, B the age in months, C the kilometres, D the fuel type, then HP, MetColor, Automatic, CC, Doors and Weight up to column J. Row 1 holds the names; rows 2 to 1420 hold the 1,419 cars. The sheets Model SLR, Model 1 and Model 2 hold the same cars with the price in column A and the explanatory variables of each model in adjacent columns. English Excel separates the arguments of a function with a comma; French Excel uses a semicolon, e.g., =INVERSE.LOI.F.DROITE(0,05;2;1408).
