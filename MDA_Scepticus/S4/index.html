Empirical Exercise IV  ·  Multivariate Data Analysis

Master Scepticus is sure that sons are, on average, exactly as tall as their fathers. The heights of 1078 fathers and sons, collected by Francis Galton, will judge him. You will compute a correlation, estimate a regression, and run your first statistical tests on its coefficients.

WINDOW 1 · EXCELDownload Galton_in_cm.xlsx with the DATA (XLSX) button at the top and open it in Excel. Compute every number there, and keep your workbook: each question builds on the previous one.
WINDOW 2 · THIS GAMEMaster Scepticus asks the questions, one after the other. Bring back the numbers, choose the interpretations, and defend them in front of him. The game never computes anything for you.

No answer is ever shown in this exercise. Every question has a HINT button, with an example Excel formula, sometimes with ??? for you to fill in. Keep trying until the Master accepts your answer.

Type numbers with a dot or a comma as the decimal separator, e.g., 0.514 or 0,514, and with three decimals.

EXCEL QUICK REFERENCE

In the file, column A holds the height of the father and column B the height of his son, in centimetres. Row 1 holds the names; rows 2 to 1079 hold the 1078 pairs. English Excel separates the arguments of a function with a comma; French Excel uses a semicolon, e.g., =COEFFICIENT.CORRELATION(A2:A1079;B2:B1079).
