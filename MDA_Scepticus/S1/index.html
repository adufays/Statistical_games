Empirical Exercise I  ·  Multivariate Data Analysis

Master Scepticus’s nephew wants to put all his savings into Apple. Master Scepticus has a better idea, and a theory about risk. 161 months of IBM and Apple returns will judge both of them. You will use averages, standard deviations, a covariance, a correlation and a portfolio of two assets.

WINDOW 1 · EXCELDownload IBM_APPLE_returns_2000_Jan_to_2017_June.xlsx with the DATA (XLSX) button at the top and open it in Excel. Compute every number there. Keep your workbook: each question builds on the previous one.
WINDOW 2 · THIS GAMEMaster Scepticus asks the questions, one after the other. Bring back the numbers, choose the interpretations, and defend them in front of him. The game never computes anything for you.

Stuck ? Every question has a HINT button. It shows an example Excel formula, sometimes with ??? for you to fill in. Type numbers with a dot or a comma as the decimal separator, e.g., -20.51 or -20,51. Returns stay in percent unless a question says otherwise. Three significant digits are always enough, e.g., 0.417, 19.3 or 0.00193.

EXCEL QUICK REFERENCE

In the file, column A holds the returns of IBM and column B those of Apple. Row 1 holds the names; rows 2 to 162 hold the 161 months, in percent. English Excel separates the arguments of a function with a comma; French Excel uses a semicolon, e.g., =COEFFICIENT.CORRELATION(A2:A162;B2:B162).
