Empirical Exercise X · Part 2  ·  Multivariate Data Analysis

Master Scepticus has run a regression of mortality on pollution and climate in 60 American cities, and he has read a negative coefficient on hydrocarbons: more pollution, fewer deaths. You will replicate his regression, watch its coefficients jump when one variable leaves, find the culprit in the correlation matrix, and repair the regression with principal components and with factors that can be interpreted. Then you will decide whether hydrocarbons and nitrogen oxides increase mortality.

WINDOW 1 · EXCELDownload DATA_PCA.xlsx with the DATA (XLSX) button at the top. The eigenvalues and eigenvectors need the function EigCov, which only exists inside the file EigCov.xlsm, available on Blackboard. Open EigCov.xlsm, enable its macros, copy the data into it, and do all your work in that workbook (save it as .xlsm): each question builds on the previous one.
WINDOW 2 · THIS GAMEMaster Scepticus asks the questions, one after the other. Bring back the numbers, read your plots, and defend your conclusions in front of him. The game never computes anything for you.

No answer is ever shown in this exercise. Every question has a HINT button, with an example Excel formula, sometimes with ??? for you to fill in. Keep trying until the Master accepts your answer.

Type numbers with a dot or a comma as the decimal separator, e.g., 36.18 or 36,18. Give two decimals: your answer is accepted within 0.01 of the exact value. Very small or very large numbers can be typed as Excel displays them, e.g., -8.54E-05, and are accepted within 1%. Counts must be exact. R² and shares are asked in percent, e.g., 12.34 for 12.34%. Every regression includes an intercept.

EXCEL QUICK REFERENCE

In the sheet Sheet1, column A holds PREC, the mean annual precipitation (inches), B JANT, the mean January temperature (°F), C HC, D NOX and E SO, the relative pollution potentials of hydrocarbons, nitrogen oxides and sulfur dioxide, and F MORT, the age-adjusted mortality rate per 100,000 inhabitants. Row 1 holds the names; rows 2 to 61 hold the 60 cities. The function EigCov works only inside EigCov.xlsm, on Blackboard. English Excel separates the arguments of a function with a comma; French Excel uses a semicolon, e.g., =LOI.STUDENT.INVERSE.BILATERALE(0,05;54).
