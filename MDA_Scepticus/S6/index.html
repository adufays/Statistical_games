Empirical Exercise VI  ·  Multivariate Data Analysis

Master Scepticus believes that inequality falls as countries grow rich, a simplified version of the Kuznets hypothesis. The Gini index and the GDP per capita of 71 countries will judge him. You will estimate a regression, check its assumptions with a QQ plot and a diagnostic plot, and test its slope in three different ways.

WINDOW 1 · EXCELDownload Kuznets_Gini_GDP_V2.xlsx with the DATA (XLSX) button at the top and open it in Excel. Compute every number and draw every plot there, and keep your workbook: each question builds on the previous one.
WINDOW 2 · THIS GAMEMaster Scepticus asks the questions, one after the other. Bring back the numbers, read your plots, and defend your conclusions in front of him. The game never computes anything for you.

No answer is ever shown in this exercise. Every question has a HINT button, with an example Excel formula, sometimes with ??? for you to fill in. Keep trying until the Master accepts your answer.

Type numbers with a dot or a comma as the decimal separator, e.g., 36.18 or 36,18, with three significant digits. Very small numbers can be typed exactly as Excel displays them, e.g., -8.54E-05.

EXCEL QUICK REFERENCE

In the file, column A holds the country, column B the Gini index and column C the GDP per capita in US dollars. Row 1 holds the names; rows 2 to 72 hold the 71 countries.
