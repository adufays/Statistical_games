CAN YOU SEE
TEN DIMENSIONS ?

Empirical Exercise X  ·  Multivariate Data Analysis

Master Scepticus claims that nobody can see a cloud of points in ten dimensions. 1,419 second-hand Toyota Corollas, each described by ten variables, will judge him. You will turn the fuel into a dummy variable, compute the correlation matrix, extract its eigenvalues and eigenvectors, draw the first two principal components, check that they are uncorrelated, and decide how many components keep 80% of the variance.

WINDOW 1 · EXCELDownload ToyotaCorolla_data.xlsx with the DATA (XLSX) button at the top. The eigenvalues and eigenvectors need the function EigCov, which only exists inside the file EigCov.xlsm, available on Blackboard. Open EigCov.xlsm, enable its macros, copy the data into it, and do all your work in that workbook (save it as .xlsm): each question builds on the previous one.
WINDOW 2 · THIS GAMEMaster Scepticus asks the questions, one after the other. Bring back the numbers, read your plots, and defend your conclusions in front of him. The game never computes anything for you.

No answer is ever shown in this exercise. Every question has a HINT button, with an example Excel formula, sometimes with ??? for you to fill in. Keep trying until the Master accepts your answer.

Type numbers with a dot or a comma as the decimal separator, e.g., 36.18 or 36,18. Give two decimals: your answer is accepted within 0.01 of the exact value. Very small or very large numbers can be typed as Excel displays them, e.g., -8.54E-05, and are accepted within 1%. Counts must be exact. Shares are asked in percent, e.g., 12.34 for 12.34%.

EXCEL QUICK REFERENCE

In the sheet ToyotaCorolla, column A holds the price in euros, B the age in months, C the kilometres, D the fuel type (Petrol or Diesel), then HP, MetColor, Automatic, CC, Doors and Weight up to column J. Row 1 holds the names; rows 2 to 1420 hold the 1,419 cars. The function EigCov works only inside EigCov.xlsm, on Blackboard. English Excel separates the arguments of a function with a comma; French Excel uses a semicolon, e.g., =SI(D2="Diesel";1;0).
